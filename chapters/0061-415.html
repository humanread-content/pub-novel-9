Sun Wukong held the fan and came near the mountain’s edge. He swung one full stroke. The Flames of Flaming Mountain quietly sank their flames; the blazes dimmed and cleared away. He was pleased and laughed with joy, then fanned once more. A rustling hiss arose, and a soft wind began to stir. He fanned a third time; the whole sky was veiled in dim cloud, and fine rain fell in drifting threads. To witness it is this verse:

Flaming Mountain lies eight hundred leagues away, and its firelight had become famed across the earth. Fire scorched the five-ingredient elixir and it could scarcely be cooked; Fire-scorch blazed through the three passes, and the Way was not made clear. At last the Ba-jiao fan poured rain and dew, Fortunate was the heavenly general’s divine aid. Let the Ox King return to Buddha and trouble cease; When water and fire join in nature, equilibrium restores itself. Now Tripitaka’s dryness and vexation were dissolved, his heart calmed and clear. The four adepts returned to refuge, thanked the vajra kings, and each turned to their jeweled mountains. The Six Ding and Six Jia rose into the air to offer protection. Passing deities scattered apart. The Heavenly King and the Crown Prince led the Ox back to the Buddha realm and returned it in custody. Only the Land God of that home mountain remained, with the rākṣasī detained beside him.

Sun Wukong said, “Rakshasa, why do you still stand here instead of going?” The Rakshasa knelt and said, “May the Great Sage look down with mercy and return my fan to me.” Zhu Bajie barked, “You insolent, ungrateful creature—you don’t even know your place. It is enough that we spared your life; what more do you want, a fan? We hauled you over the mountain, we are not going to go home and buy snacks with sold-for-money cash. After all this strain and effort, why still ask for it? It is raining in drizzles—go home at once.” The Rakshasa knelt again and said, “The Great Sage had once asked me to fan the flames away and then return it. In this case I deeply regret my lateness in repentance. It was only because I was brazen and careless that I caused men to be assembled and disturbed. We, too, are trying to cultivate the path of men, only not yet reaching the true fruit. Seeing now that my true form has been exposed and has turned toward the west, I dare not act rashly again. I beg that you grant this fan, let me reform from this moment, and depart to cultivate and preserve my life.” The Earth God said, “Great Sage, this young lady knows the art of extinguishing flames. If you cut off the source, and return her fan, this little spirit can stay here in peace and save the people of this land, whose sustenance is from blood offerings. That would be true grace.” Sun Wukong said, “When I spoke with the villagers just now, they said that this mountain, once fanned to suppress fire, yields a year’s grain and then the flames flare up again. How can the root be truly cut out?” The Rakshasa said, “If you must cut the root, then simply fan it continuously forty-nine times; it will never burn again.”

On hearing that, Sun Wukong took the fan and used all his strength, then fanned toward the mountain-top forty-nine times in succession. A heavy rain poured over the mountain. It was indeed a precious secret: wherever there was fire, it rained; where there was no fire, the sky stayed clear. The Master and his three disciples stood in the fireless spot and were not drenched.

After sitting there for the whole night, they did not begin packing until the next morning, then gathered up the horse gear and luggage, and returned the fan to the Rakshasa. He said, “If I did not give it back, people would say my word meant nothing. Take your fan back to the mountain and make no trouble again. Now that you have obtained a human form, be off and spared.” The Rakshasa took the fan, chanted a spell, shaped it into a tiny apricot leaf and put it into her mouth. She thanked all the saints, concealed her former name, and withdrew to cultivate; later she attained the true fruit and became known for ten thousand ages in the Buddhist Canon. The Rakshasa and the Earth God both thanked them in gratitude and then escorted them out. Sun Wukong, Zhu Bajie, and Sha Wujing protected Tripitaka and moved on. Their bodies were truly cooled, and their feet felt refreshed. Indeed it is rightly said:

Once Kan and Li are harmonized, true essence is complete;\ where water and fire are held in equal balance, the Great Path is fulfilled.

It is still unknown how many years it would be before they finally returned to the Eastern Land; listen to the resolution in the next chapter.
